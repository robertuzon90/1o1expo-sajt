What to send a stand builder: the stand brief

CategoryBriefingAuthorRobert UzonLast updatedOctober 2026Reading time5 min
ON THIS PAGEFor a first quoteThe full briefFiles that helpCommon mistakesBrief templateCommon questions
KEY TAKEAWAYS
  • For a first quote, four things are enough: the fair, the stand size, the open sides and your email.
  • A good brief explains what visitors should do at your stand, not only what it should look like.
  • Share a budget range. It does not raise the price, it lets the design fit what you want to spend.
  • Send the organiser's floor plan and technical guidelines early. They decide what can be built.

What we need for a first quote

You don't need a finished brief to get started. With these four details we can check your space and come back with a first design and price:

  • Fair and city: e.g. Hannover Messe, Hannover
  • Stand size: in m², e.g. 6 × 8 m
  • Open sides: or simply the floor plan from your booking. What are open sides?
  • Your email: so we can send you the design and price

Everything below makes the first design closer to what you have in mind, so fewer rounds of changes are needed. Missing details are fine. We sort them out together.

The full brief, step by step

A complete brief covers six areas. You can send them in any order, in an email, a document or a short call.

The fair and your space

Where and when, and what the organiser allows.

  • Fair name, city and dates, plus the build-up and dismantling days
  • Hall and stand number
  • Stand size and open sides, or the floor plan from your booking
  • The organiser's technical guidelines: maximum height, hanging points, pillars and approval deadlines

What the stand should do

The most important part of the brief.

  • Who you want to reach: existing clients, new leads, distributors or press
  • What visitors should do: book a meeting, try a product, watch a demo, leave their details
  • One or two things visitors should remember after they leave
  • How many people will work on the stand at the same time

What the stand must hold

The practical list.

  • Products to show, with sizes and weights, especially heavy or large ones
  • Screens, counters, shelves or display cases
  • Meeting area: open tables or a closed room, and for how many people
  • Storage room, coat space, a small kitchen or a bar

Your brand

So the stand looks like you.

  • Logo as a vector file (AI, EPS, SVG or PDF)
  • Brand guidelines with colours and fonts
  • Photos and key messages you want on the walls
  • Photos of past stands, with what you liked and what you didn't

Budget

A range is enough.

  • A budget range for the whole project

Dates and decisions

So nothing is late.

  • When you need the first design
  • Who approves the design on your side
  • Organiser deadlines for stand drawings, electrical orders and other services

Common mistakes

No budget at allWithout a range, the first design is a guess. It may be far above or below what you want to spend, and you lose a round of changes.
Only a logo and "make it nice"The look is the easy part. What visitors should do at the stand decides the layout, so say it.
Forgetting the organiser's deadlinesLarger and special stands often need approved drawings weeks before build-up, and many fairs add a surcharge to service orders placed after the deadline.
References without a reasonA photo of a stand you like helps most when you say why: the colours, the openness, the meeting area or the lighting.

Copy-paste brief template

Copy this into an email, fill in what you know and leave the rest blank.

Fair: [name, city, dates]
Hall / stand number: [ ]
Stand size and open sides: [e.g. 48 m², corner, 2 open sides]

Goal: [what visitors should do at the stand]
Audience: [who you want to meet]
Must-haves: [meeting area for 6, storage room, 2 screens, ...]
Products to show: [items, sizes, weights]

Brand files: [logo, guidelines, photos – attached]
Budget range: [from – to, for the whole project]
First design needed by: [date]
Approval on our side: [name, role]

Common questions

Should I share my budget?+

Yes, if you can. A range does not raise the price. It tells us which materials and details make sense, so the first design fits what you want to spend and you avoid a round of changes.

How detailed does the brief need to be?+

Less than you think. Four details are enough for a first quote. The more you tell us about goals and must-haves, the closer the first design gets.

Can I send pictures of stands I like?+

Yes, they help a lot. Add one line on what you like about each one, for example the colours, the open layout or the lighting. We use them as inspiration, not as a copy.

What if my floor plan is not final yet?+

Send what you have. We can start from the size and the open sides and adjust the design once the organiser confirms the final space.
