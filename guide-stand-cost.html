What drives the cost of an exhibition stand

There is no honest price per m². Here is what actually moves the price of a stand, what you pay the organiser on top, and where you can save without it showing.

By the 1o1 expo team · Updated October 2026
ON THIS PAGETwo separate billsWhat moves the priceWhat matters lessCosts that come lateWhere to saveCommon questions
KEY TAKEAWAYS
  • You pay two separate bills: one for the stand, one to the organiser for the space and venue services.
  • Size, design complexity and materials move the stand price the most.
  • The stand type mainly changes the space rent, not the cost of the stand itself.
  • Late changes and late orders are the most avoidable extra costs.

Two separate bills

When you exhibit, the money goes to two places. Knowing which is which makes quotes easier to compare, because some builders include items that others leave out.

The standPaid to your stand builder. With us, one all‑inclusive price.
  • Design and visuals
  • Walls, flooring and furniture
  • Lighting and electrics on the stand
  • Printed graphics and branding
  • Transport to the venue
  • Build-up and dismantling
The organiserPaid directly to the fair, priced by the organiser.
  • Space rent per m²
  • Electricity, water and internet connections
  • Hanging points for anything suspended from the ceiling
  • Mandatory fees, such as a media or catalogue package and waste disposal
  • Cleaning, security and extra build-up days, if you order them

At German fairs you will usually also see a small AUMA fee per m², charged by the organiser for the German trade fair association.

What moves the stand price

These are the things that change the price of the stand itself, roughly in the order they matter.

1SizeMore square metres means more floor, walls, graphics, transport and build-up time. It is the first thing that moves the price.
2Design complexity and heightCurved walls, closed ceilings, raised platforms and tall structures take more production hours. Above certain heights, and for double-deck stands, many fairs require approval, structural calculations and inspection fees.
3Materials and finishReal wood, lacquered surfaces, glass and custom-built furniture cost more than standard panels and printed fabric. The difference shows most up close, where visitors stand.
4GraphicsLarge printed walls, many separate panels and special prints like backlit fabric or 3D letters add up. Fewer, larger graphics usually look better and cost less.
5Lighting and AVScreens, LED walls and special lighting are often a large share of the stand budget, especially when they are custom sized.
6Rooms and extrasA closed meeting room, a storage room, a kitchen or a bar each need walls, doors, power and sometimes water.
7Transport and build-upDistance to the venue, how many trucks are needed, hall access and how many days the crew needs on site.

What matters less than you think

The stand typeRow, corner, peninsula or island mainly changes the space rent you pay the organiser. A modern row stand can cost more to build than a simple island.
Fewer wallsAn open stand has fewer walls, but every side is seen, so counters, furniture and the back of every element must look finished.
The cityTransport distance matters, but the stand itself is built to the same standard whether the fair is in Milan, Paris or Frankfurt.

Costs that come late

Most surprises on the final invoice come from timing, not from the design.

Changes after approvalOnce production starts, changing walls, sizes or graphics means remaking parts that are already built.
Late orders to the organiserMany fairs add a surcharge to electricity, rigging and other services ordered after the deadline. At some German fairs it is 20 to 25 percent.
Approvals and inspectionsSpecial structures, like double-deck stands or very tall walls, need approved drawings weeks before build-up. Missing or late documents can mean extra fees.
Extra build-up timeStarting earlier or dismantling later than the standard days is usually booked and paid separately.

Where you can save without it showing

  • Share a budget range early, so the first design already fits it.
  • Spend where visitors look: the front, the counter and the main wall. Keep storage rooms and back areas simple.
  • Use fewer, larger graphics instead of many small panels.
  • Check the height limits and approval rules early. Staying under them avoids extra calculations and fees.
  • Approve the design on time and order the organiser's services before the deadline.
  • Decide early who signs off on your side, so production is not waiting.

Common questions

Why don't you publish a price per m²?+

Because there is no honest one. Two stands of the same size can differ several times in price, depending on design, materials and what the stand has to hold. Tell us your budget and we design the best stand for it.

What is included in your price?+

Design, walls, flooring, furniture, lighting and electrics on the stand, printed graphics, transport, build-up and dismantling. Floor space, electricity, water and other venue services are paid directly to the organiser.

Will the price change after I approve the design?+

Every change is priced before you approve it, and from your yes the price is fixed.

Can you work to a fixed budget?+

Yes. Share your budget range and we design to it. If something does not fit, we tell you before you approve, not after.
