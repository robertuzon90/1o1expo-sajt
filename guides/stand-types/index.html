Row, corner, peninsula or island: which stand should you book?

Author1o1 expo teamCategoryStand typesLast updatedOctober 2026Reading time4 min
ON THIS PAGEQuick comparisonRow standCorner standPeninsula standIsland standHow to chooseCommon questions
KEY TAKEAWAYS
  • The number of open sides is set by the organiser when you book your space.
  • More open sides mean more visibility, more design freedom and usually a higher space rent from the organiser.
  • Check the organiser's technical guidelines before you design: wall heights, hanging points and approvals differ from fair to fair.

SPACE RENT VS. STAND COST

You pay two separate things. The space rent goes to the organiser, per m², and most fairs charge more per m² for every extra open side. In the price lists we checked, an island cost anywhere from a few percent to around a third more per m² than a row stand, so check the price list of your fair. Some fairs also charge for the upper floor of a double‑deck stand.

The stand cost depends on the design, materials and finish, not on the stand type. A modern row stand can cost more than a simple island.

01 · 1 OPEN SIDE

Row stand · Reihenstand

Your stand sits in a row between neighbours, with walls on the back and both sides and one side open to the aisle.

WHAT WORKS WELL
  • Usually the lowest space rent per m²
  • Lots of wall space for graphics, screens and shelving
  • Easy to add a small storage room
WHAT TO WATCH
  • Visitors can only reach you from one aisle
  • Less visible from a distance, so your back wall has to work hard

Best for: Exhibitors with a smaller budget, a clear product range and a strong brand wall.

02 · 2 OPEN SIDES

Corner stand · Eckstand

Your stand sits at the end of a row, with two walls and two sides open to the aisles.

WHAT WORKS WELL
  • Visitors reach you from two aisles
  • More visibility than a row stand for a modest step up in space rent
  • Still has two walls for graphics and storage
WHAT TO WATCH
  • Both open sides need to invite people in, so the layout takes more planning
  • Many organisers charge a supplement for corner space

Best for: Exhibitors who want more foot traffic without the space rent of a peninsula or island.

03 · 3 OPEN SIDES

Peninsula stand · Kopfstand

Your stand sits at the head of a row, with one back wall and three sides open.

WHAT WORKS WELL
  • Very visible, with visitors coming from three directions
  • One strong back wall for your brand and a storage room
  • Room for product displays, a meeting area and a reception desk
WHAT TO WATCH
  • The back wall is shared with the neighbour behind you, so its height and finish follow the organiser's rules
  • Layout has to work from three directions

Best for: Mid-size and larger exhibitors who want presence and a clear brand wall.

04 · 4 OPEN SIDES

Island stand · Inselstand

A free-standing stand, open on all four sides, with no direct neighbours.

WHAT WORKS WELL
  • The biggest presence in the hall, visible from every aisle
  • Full freedom in the layout, including closed meeting rooms
  • Can be built as a double-deck stand where the hall allows it
WHAT TO WATCH
  • The most complex to plan, and usually the highest space rent per m²
  • Height limits, hanging points and double-deck structures need organiser approval and often structural calculations

Best for: Larger exhibitors who want the strongest presence and room for meetings, demos and hospitality.

How to choose

Start with what you want visitors to do at your stand, not with the floor plan. A few questions help:

  1. 01Do you need to show large products or machines?Open sides give people room to walk around them.
  2. 02Do you hold many meetings?A peninsula or island has room for a closed meeting area.
  3. 03Is your brand new to this audience?More open sides mean more people see you from the aisles.
  4. 04What is your budget for the whole stand?Space, design, build and transport all grow with the stand.

Common questions

Is a corner stand worth the extra space rent?+

Often, yes. The corner supplement is usually a modest share of the space rent, and a second aisle can bring noticeably more visitors past your stand. If your stand is mainly a brand wall with a small counter, a row stand can be enough.

Can a peninsula stand be double‑deck?+

Sometimes. It depends on the hall and the organiser's rules. A double-deck stand needs approval and structural calculations, some fairs set a minimum stand size, and the upper floor may be charged as extra space. Island stands are the most common choice for a double-deck.

Can I change my stand type after booking?+

Only through the organiser. Corner, peninsula and island spaces are limited and usually go first, so ask early. If you already have your space, we design for the open sides you have.

Does the stand type change your price?+

Not by itself. Our price depends on the size, design, materials and finish of your stand. The stand type changes the space rent you pay to the organiser.
